# Phase 1 input: every identifier this codebase defines

Extracted by AST from `talaria/`, `tests/`, `run.py` and `scripts/` — function
and class names, parameters, and assignment and loop targets. Excludes
builtins, names already in the rename map, names of two characters or fewer,
and `test_*` method names.

`<count>\t<identifier>`, most frequent first.

## Why this exists

Phase 1's acceptance criteria were: no accented characters, and no Portuguese
function words. Both miss **unaccented Portuguese identifiers**, and there are
roughly 727 occurrences of them — `estado` (138 including attribute reads),
`texto` (98), `fim` (62), `pasta` (55), `destino` (40), `valor`, `faixa`,
`corpo`, `conta`, `peca`, `itens`, `origem`, `trecho`, `dados`, `alvo`,
`inicio`, `lista`, `chave`, `tamanho`, `ordem`, `duracao`, `titulo`.

Neither criterion fires on any of them. The rename map does not contain them
either, because the Task 1 brief asked for top-level definitions and constants
and these are mostly parameters and locals.

This is the fourth time in this project that a verification has been thorough
about the categories its author imagined and blind to one they had not. The
answer is not a longer keyword list — it is this inventory: a finite,
enumerable set where every member gets a verdict, so completeness is checkable
rather than asserted.

## How to triage

For each identifier, decide: is it an English word, a standard abbreviation, or
a domain term a reader of English would understand?

- **Keep** — English (`path`, `segs`, `slug`, `start`, `dur`, `img`, `buf`),
  or conventional (`self`, `setUp`, `args`, `req`, `cur`, `out`), or a
  third-party name we did not choose (`boto3_mock`).
- **Rename** — Portuguese. Add a row to `rename-map.tsv` with the right scope.

Judge the word, not its length. `pares` is Portuguese for pairs; `pontos` is
points; `corpo` is body; `erros` is errors. Some are ambiguous across the two
languages (`total`, `item`, `video`, `local`) — those are fine as they stand,
and say so rather than renaming for the sake of it.
